# BLACK MERIDIAN — World Bible

**Canonical reference. Version 1.0. Owned by the Story team.**

This document is the authority on fiction for the project. Where another
system invents a name, a place, a manufacturer or a faction detail that
contradicts this file, this file wins and the other system conforms.

Everything here is invented. Nothing in Meridian Basin maps onto a real
country, a real conflict, a real people or a real war. Where the geography
resembles the physical world it does so because rain shadows and tectonic
grabens work the same everywhere; where the politics resemble anything, it is
because paperwork behaves the same everywhere too.

**Tone contract.** Grounded, restrained, morally uncomfortable. Nobody in this
world says *tango down*. Nobody is having a good time. The player is not a
hero, and the game does not pretend otherwise — it just keeps the accounts
honestly and lets the player read them.

---

## 1. THE BASIN

### 1.1 Physical geography

Meridian Basin is a tectonic graben — a block of crust that dropped between two
faults — running roughly 380 km north-east to south-west. The floor sits
between 240 and 420 m above sea level. It has no outlet to the sea.

The ocean is to the **east**. Between the ocean and the basin stands the
**Sarrahun**, a cordillera whose crest runs 3,600–4,100 m. This single wall
explains almost everything about the region:

| Zone | Elevation | Rainfall | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kavesk Littoral (coastal plain) | 0–200 m | 1,100 mm | Humid, cane and container yards |
| Sarrahun Escarpment (seaward flank) | 900–2,200 m | **3,200 mm** | Permanent cloud forest. Fogged 210 days a year |
| Halloran Crest (the pass) | 2,050 m | 900 mm | Wind, scree, tunnel mouths, no trees |
| Ustari Gate (river gorge, landward) | 1,400–2,400 m | 600 mm | Snowmelt, terraces, the reservoir |
| Basin Floor | 380–700 m | 90 mm | Thorn scrub, gravel, resettlement blocks |
| Ka'sun Flat (terminal salt pan) | 240 m | **40 mm** | Salt crust, brine ponds, nothing |

Moist air comes off the ocean, is forced up the Sarrahun, dumps its water on
the seaward flank, and arrives in the basin dry. The seaward flank is cloud
forest so wet the trees grow their own soil in the air. Sixty kilometres away,
over a ridge, the ground has not been rained on since 2019.

The **Ustari River** rises on the landward slope from snowmelt and from fog
that drips off the crest vegetation. It runs west-north-west across the basin
floor and dies in the Ka'sun Flat, where it evaporates into brine. There is no
outflow. Everything the river carries — silt, salt, and for the last decade a
measurable concentration of industrial solvent — stays in the basin.

### 1.2 The two seasons

The Basin does not have four seasons. It has two, and both are named for what
they do to you.

**The Fog** (November–April). The escarpment is socked in from 900 m upward,
visibility under 40 m for weeks. The reservoir fills. The switchback road above
the Cut washes out at least twice. Aircraft do not fly the escarpment. This is
when work gets done that should not be seen from orbit.

**The Ossun** (May–October). A hot katabatic wind pours off the crest into the
basin — 22 to 30 m/s in the gusts, on 60 to 90 days of the season. It lifts the
salt crust off the Ka'sun Flat and turns the sky the colour of wet cement. It
grounds light aircraft, blinds optics, buries roads, and drives people mad in a
way everyone in the Basin acknowledges and nobody discusses. Old hands say the
Ossun *"asks after you"*.

*(Weather team: `sandstorm` = the Ossun. `fog` = escarpment cloud, and it should
be at its worst between 900 and 2,200 m. `heavyRain` belongs to the seaward
flank and the littoral, essentially never to the flat.)*

### 1.3 The map, in one paragraph

Everything in the Basin is strung along a single axis, because there is only
one way in. From the sea: the port at **Kavesk**. Thirty-four kilometres of
switchback and eleven kilometres of tunnel — **Halloran's Cut** — climb the
escarpment to the crest at 2,050 m, with the shanty city of the same name
packed into the cutting and up both walls. Above the Cut, in the cool air at
1,400 m, sits the **Verrick Residence**. Forty kilometres north along the fog
belt is the **Ondai Biological Station**. West over the crest and down: the
**Ustari Gate** dam at 1,900 m, then the long descent to the basin floor, then
210 km of nothing to **Number Six Lift** out on the salt.

One road. One rail line. One tunnel. Every gram, litre, kilowatt and person in
the Basin passes a point where somebody can stand.

That is the entire political history of the region, expressed as terrain.

## 2. HISTORY

Compressed, because the game should deliver this in fragments, never as a
lecture. Dates matter; a briefing that cites 1911 or 1984 should be citing
something true.

**Before 1889.** The **Marrek**, a confederation of valley councils on the
seaward flank, farming terraces in the fog belt. On the basin side, salt
caravans crossing the flat twice a year. The two economies met at the crest and
did not much like each other.

**1889 — the Concession.** The **Meridian Concession**, a chartered joint-stock
company registered in Europe, is granted ninety-nine years of mineral rights
over "the interior basin and its approaches" by the coastal **Kingdom of
Alluvine**, which had never surveyed the interior and was paid in advance. No
Marrek council was consulted, and none was aware for eleven years.

**1901–1911 — Halloran's Cut.** The company drives a rail cutting and eleven
kilometres of tunnel through the crest. The recorded death toll is 4,100. The
real figure is unknown, because the company recorded contract labour and not
the rest. The surveyor, **Emeric Halloran**, also runs the concession
boundary — a straight line, bearing 041°, ignoring every river, ridge and
village on it.

That line is now an international border. Three states' worth of people are on
the wrong side of a bearing a man wrote down in a notebook in 1911.

**1912–1958 — the company century.** Nitrate, potash, borate. Company towns
numbered **Reach One** through **Reach Nine**. A hospital, nine schools, a
private police force, and a wage paid in scrip redeemable only at company
stores. Two generations of Marrek families move down off the terraces to work
the Cut, and never move back.

**1958 — independence.** The **Alluvine Republic**. The Concession is
nationalised on paper. In practice the company keeps operating under a
management contract until 1974, and keeps the surveys.

**1974 — Ondai.** A university consortium builds a biological station in the
fog belt on donor money to catalogue the escarpment's endemics. Twelve years of
genuine, careful science. Six thousand pressed specimens. Nobody involved is a
villain, which is why the place is so sad now.

**1979–1986 — the Ustari Gate.** A 148 m arch dam, 1,180 MW, financed by a
development bank on the argument that cheap power would industrialise the
interior. Three valley towns — **Ollant**, **Tenkai** and **Sevra** — are
flooded. Eleven thousand people are moved into resettlement blocks in the Cut.
Compensation is paid, in full, to the Confederacy councils. Not one household
receives any of it.

This is the founding wound of the modern Basin. Everything violent that happens
after 1986 can be traced to eleven thousand people who were moved into concrete
and told to be grateful for the electricity.

**1989–1998 — the Basin War.** Not an ethnic war. Not a religious war. A war
about a **tariff**. When the Alluvine treasury raises the transit levy on the
Cut by 140% in 1989, the highland councils, the Kavesk harbour pilots and the
company's remaining local managers each discover a reason to shoot. It runs
nine years and kills roughly 40,000 people, the large majority of hunger and
cholera in the resettlement blocks rather than of gunfire.

Everyone in the Basin now describes this war in the language of identity and
ancient grievance. Everyone in the Basin is lying, mostly to themselves. It was
a fight about a tax, and the people who started it are alive and comfortable.

**1998 — the Halloran Line Accords.** Signed in a hotel in a third country by
people who had mostly not been to the Basin. The Accords freeze the line,
create the **Basin Interim Administration**, and postpone the sovereignty
question for "a period not exceeding ten years."

**2003 — Ondai closes.** Funding ends mid-cycle. The staff evacuate over one
weekend and leave the herbarium, the cold room and a decade of unpublished
data. The last director's note is still pinned in the mess: *back Monday.*

**2009 — the ninety-nine years expire.** **Meridian Concession Holdings SA**,
now a Luxembourg shell with four employees and a very good law firm, files for
international arbitration, claiming the expiry was procured by expropriation.
The arbitration begins.

**2011.** The BIA joint commission holds its last plenary meeting. It has never
been formally dissolved. Its budget still clears.

**c. 2014 — Nine.** The chemistry is not new. **BX-9**, a nitazene-class
opioid, was first synthesised in 1967 in a Concession laboratory as a
veterinary anaesthetic and shelved after trials for "unacceptable respiratory
depression." Someone found the file. The first kitchens were in the Cut. They
moved up into the fog belt when it became obvious that 210 days of cloud a year
beat any camouflage netting ever manufactured.

**Now.** Year fourteen of the arbitration. The tribunal is scheduled to sit in
**fourteen months**. Every armed group, every ministry, every donor and every
law firm in the Basin is working to a calendar that ends on that date, and most
of the people doing the shooting have never heard of it.

## 3. WHY NO STATE CONTROLS THE BASIN

The lazy answer is "the government is weak." That is not the answer here.

The Basin is ungoverned because **the ambiguity is worth more than the
resolution, to everyone with the power to resolve it.**

- **The Alluvine Republic** holds legal sovereignty over the Cut and the
  littoral. It collects the transit tariff at Kavesk without paying for the
  administration of anything above 200 m. Sovereignty over the interior would
  cost it money it does not have and casualties it cannot survive politically.
  Its army has not gone past the crest since 1998, and the last time it tried,
  the Ossun grounded its helicopters for nineteen days.
- **The Marrek Confederacy** — the highland councils — holds a claim to the
  interior worth a great deal unresolved and rather less settled. Every year
  the claim sits open, it accrues arbitral interest.
- **Meridian Concession Holdings SA** needs the facts on the ground to support
  a valuation. A functioning, governed, taxed Basin is an expensive thing to
  have lost. A wrecked one is a cheap thing to have lost. The company's
  interest, stated in no document anywhere, is that the Basin should look
  exactly as bad as possible on the day the tribunal sits.
- **The Basin Interim Administration** — 4,100 employees, a flag, a compound in
  Kavesk and no soldiers — exists because of the unresolved question. It issues
  mining licences nobody honours and publishes a quarterly humanitarian
  bulletin nobody reads. Its mandate, and its salaries, end when the question
  is answered.
- **The armed groups** need the absence of law the way a fish needs water.

So there is no conspiracy. There is only a set of actors, each rationally
preferring that nothing be decided. The Basin is not a failure of governance.
It is a **successful equilibrium** that happens to kill people.

## 4. THE ECONOMY

Five revenue streams. Each one maps to a site and to a faction, which is why
the campaign layer can simulate them.

### 4.1 Power — the real currency

The Ustari Gate is rated 1,180 MW and delivers about 640: two units have been
offline since 2016, the intakes are silting, and nobody can finance a rebuild
while the ownership is in arbitration. What it does deliver, it sells at the
switchyard, in cash, per megawatt-hour, to whoever shows up with money.

Everything downstream is a customer. The port cranes. The brine pumps. The
kitchens in the fog belt, which need enormous power to run reflux condensers.
And the Cut, which does not pay at all — 62,000 people running illegal taps off
a 220 kV line, an arrangement that kills between forty and seventy people a
year and that nobody has ever seriously proposed ending.

**Power is the Basin's central bank.** The faction that holds the switchyard is
not rich; it is *systemically important*, which is better. Everyone needs them,
including their enemies, which is why the dam has survived three sieges.

### 4.2 Nine — the export

Basin-made. Synthesised in the fog belt, pressed and cut in the Cut, shipped
through Kavesk in potash containers.

The cut is **bittern** — the leftover magnesium-chloride liquor from the brine
ponds at Number Six Lift. It is free, locally abundant, and it turns the pressed
wafers grey. Hence the street vocabulary: *clean nine* is nearly white; *salted*
is grey and hits like a brick. Users buy by the **card** — a foil sheet of ten
wafers.

Wholesale at the wharf: about **$4,100/kg**. Retail abroad: forty times that.
The entire Basin's cut of a product worth billions is a number that would not
buy a mid-size European city a tram network.

Note for everyone writing this world: **the money does not stay here.** The
Basin is the dirty end of somebody else's margin. Nobody in the Cut is rich.

### 4.3 Brine — the legal export

Potash, borate, and lithium carbonate from the Ka'sun Flat. The only export
with paperwork, and the only reason the arbitration is worth running: the
lithium is what makes the concession valuable, and the concession is what the
tribunal will value.

### 4.4 Transit — the Cut

Every tonne moving through Halloran's Cut pays. Officially it pays an Alluvine
customs tariff. Unofficially it also pays the Pilotage (loading), the Rungs
(passage through the settlement), whoever currently holds the upper tunnel
mouth, and a "facilitation" line item at the customs house itself.

A container arriving at Kavesk pays five separate authorities before it reaches
the crest, and only one of those five will give you a receipt.

### 4.5 Donor money

About **$180 million a year** flows into the Basin through the BIA and through
NGOs, principally the **Halloran Trust for Basin Development**. It builds
clinics, it runs a water programme, it pays for the only functioning ambulance
service above 900 m. It also moves in convoys, and convoys get taxed, and the
tax funds the people the convoys are protected from.

Every honest aid worker in the Basin knows this arithmetic and continues
anyway, because the alternative arithmetic is worse. This is not hypocrisy. It
is the actual moral shape of the place, and the game should respect it.

## 5. THE SIX SITES

The World team's six archetypes, placed, named and given a job. Each site has
at least two names, because in the Basin **everything has two names and the one
you use tells people who you are.**

### 5.1 Port Kavesk — *industrial port*

**Names:** *Kavesk* (Marrek, the original fishing anchorage) · *Meridian Wharf*
(Concession) · *Port Kavesk* (Alluvine official) · *the yard* (everyone).

**Where:** Sea level, the littoral, at the foot of the escarpment.

**What it is:** Two container berths, a bulk potash loader, the Concession's
1913 **nitrate works** — now doing tolling chemistry for anyone with a purchase
order and no questions — a tank farm, a BIA compound with a helipad it cannot
afford to use, and the Alluvine customs house.

**Economic function:** The mouth. Everything the Basin sells leaves here and
everything it needs arrives here. The customs house is the most profitable
building in the country and it is not close.

**Political function:** The one place where the Alluvine Republic is genuinely
sovereign, which means it is the one place where the fiction of a state is
maintained, which means everyone conducts their most respectable business here.

**Held by:** the Pilotage. Contested by nobody, because nobody wants the port to
stop working.

**Texture:** Rust, sodium light, the smell of ammonia off the works. Gantry
cranes that were new in 1978. A line of trucks that never gets shorter. Somebody
is always welding.

---

### 5.2 Halloran Cut — *favela block*

**Names:** *Ketturai* (Marrek, "the ladder") · *Concession Reach Four*
(company/official) · *Halloran Cut* (maps) · *the Cut* (everyone, always).

**Where:** In and around the rail cutting, from 340 m up to the tunnel mouth at
2,050 m. The city climbs both walls of the cutting on stairs.

**What it is:** Approximately **62,000 people** with no legal address. Concrete
resettlement blocks from 1984 at the bottom, forty years of self-built accretion
above them, all of it hanging off the cutting walls and connected by staircases,
ladders and roof routes rather than streets. There are perhaps nine places a
vehicle can go. Electricity is stolen; water is carried; the drainage is the
cutting itself, which is why cholera returns every Fog.

**Economic function:** Labour. The port, the works, the kitchens and the road
all run on people who live here. Also retail: the Basin's own consumption of
Nine happens on these stairs.

**Political function:** The demographic centre of gravity. Any faction that
wants numbers comes here to get them. Any faction that wants to hide a hundred
fighters can do so among 62,000 civilians in an afternoon.

**Held by:** the Rungs, street by street. Taxed by the Pilotage. Supplied by the
Table.

**Texture:** Corrugated sheet, painted concrete in colours chosen because that
was the paint available. Cables in ropes overhead. Music from four directions.
Children everywhere, at every hour. It is the most alive place in the Basin and
the game should make the player feel awful about fighting in it.

---

### 5.3 The Verrick Residence — *cartel villa*

**Names:** *Verrick* (universal) · *the Residence* (formal) · *Ollan's house*
(Marrek, sardonic — Ollan was a drowned town, and the joke is that the house is
where the compensation went).

**Where:** 1,400 m on the seaward flank, above the Cut, in the cool air.

**What it is:** Built in 1911 for the Concession's resident director, because
Europeans could not sleep at sea level. Forty rooms, a terrace with a view down
1,400 m to the ocean, a colonnade, and the only maintained lawn in the Basin —
irrigated, at ruinous expense, as a statement.

**Economic function:** A **clearing house**. Verrick is where the Table meets:
where the wholesale price of Nine is agreed, where freight rates with the
Pilotage are negotiated, where the customs director comes to dinner. No product
is ever on the premises. What is on the premises is arithmetic.

**Political function:** The Basin's actual capital. More decisions that affect
more lives are made on that terrace than in any ministry.

**Held by:** the Verrick Table.

**Texture:** Deliberately, insultingly civilised. Staff in white. Glassware.
Security that is *not* obvious, because a visible garrison would advertise
weakness, and because a firefight at Verrick would be catastrophic for
everyone's business. There are perhaps eight genuinely dangerous men on the
property and forty people who cook, drive, garden and clean.

The tactical problem at Verrick is not the guns. It is the cooks.

---

### 5.4 Ondai Biological Station — *jungle research station*

**Names:** *Ondai* (Marrek, the ridge) · *the Station* · *the kitchen* (trade).

**Where:** 1,650 m in the fog belt, 40 km north of the Cut, at the end of eleven
kilometres of switchback the state stopped maintaining in 2004.

**What it is:** Twelve buildings on stilts. A herbarium with 6,000 pressed
specimens still in their cabinets. A cold room. A generator house. Mist nets
still strung between two trees, rotted through. And, since about 2016, an
industrial synthesis line running BX-9 at roughly 40 kg a month.

**Economic function:** Production. The station has the three things a kitchen
needs — grid power off the dam line, unlimited clean water, and a road nobody
watches — plus one thing money cannot buy, which is 210 days a year of cloud
that no satellite, aircraft or drone can see through.

**Political function:** It is a Table member's private asset, and therefore a
constant source of friction inside the Table, because the others suspect he is
under-declaring output. Kirravai has burned it twice and will again.

**Texture:** Wet. Everything is wet. Moss on the handrails, fungus on the
document boxes, the constant drip of condensate off leaves. Reagent drums stored
on herbarium shelves, alphabetically, because whoever stacked them had no idea
what else to do with the labels. Someone's 1990s field notebook, still legible,
on a bench next to a hotplate.

---

### 5.5 Ustari Gate — *mountain hydro facility*

**Names:** *Ustari Gate* · *the Gate* · *the dam* · *the grave* (Kirravai only,
and it is not a metaphor to them).

**Where:** 1,900 m, in the gorge on the landward side of the crest.

**What it is:** A 148 m double-curvature arch dam. Six penstocks. A machine hall
in rock. A 220 kV switchyard on a bench above the tailrace, which is the part
that actually matters. A staff village of 300 built in 1981 that now houses
about 900.

**Economic function:** The central bank, as above. Also the largest single
employer above 900 m.

**Political function:** The reason the Ustari Watch exists, and the reason
Kirravai exists, and the reason the two of them will never, ever come to terms.

**Texture:** Cold. Wind through the switchyard insulators makes a sound
everybody who works there hates. Painted floor markings from 1983, repainted.
The men on the crest are in their fifties and have been doing this since they
were nineteen.

**The detail that matters:** in the Ossun, the reservoir drops eleven metres,
and the church tower of Tenkai comes out of the water. Kirravai holds services
on it. There are photographs; they circulate; the Watch confiscates them.

---

### 5.6 Number Six Lift — *desert outpost*

**Names:** *Number Six Lift* (Concession) · *Sixlift* (universal) · *Ka'sun Six*
(BIA licence documents).

**Where:** 390 m, 210 km out on the Ka'sun Flat.

**What it is:** A brine pump station — one of nine the Concession numbered, and
the only one still running. Evaporation ponds out to the horizon in geometric
rectangles. A tank farm. A workshop. And 1,400 m of graded dirt that is the only
fuelled airstrip in the interior.

**Economic function:** Brine, on paper. In practice, **the air bridge**. Sixlift
is how anything gets into or out of the Basin without going through the Cut, and
that makes it worth more than the potash by an order of magnitude.

**Political function:** It sits on **Concession Parcel 41**, which is why
Margrave garrisons it: not to defend the potash, but to maintain a documented,
photographed, continuously staffed physical presence on a parcel whose title is
before a tribunal.

**Texture:** White. Salt crust to the horizon in every direction, and the
horizon is a mirage. The heat comes off the pan in sheets. There is no cover
anywhere and both sides know it. When the Ossun runs, you cannot see the far end
of the airstrip and the salt gets into everything, including the bolt of your
rifle, including your teeth.

## 6. THE FACTIONS

Six armed groups. Each one has a coherent internal logic: a thing it wants, a
way it pays for that, a way it fights, and a specific relationship to the people
who live where it operates.

**The tactical differentiation is the point.** A Table gunman, a Kirravai cell
and a Margrave operator should be identifiable within four seconds of contact,
from behaviour alone, with the HUD off.

---

### 6.1 THE VERRICK TABLE — `cartel`

> *"There is no organisation called the Verrick Table. There is a table, at
> Verrick, and the men who sit at it agree a price."* — BIA Narcotics Liaison
> Office, cable 2019/114

**What it actually is:** a cartel in the original, literal sense — an
**association of independent producers who fix a price and enforce the
agreement**. Nine or ten principals, each with his own kitchens, his own
chemists, his own gunmen, his own arrangements. They are not a hierarchy. They
are a *cartel meeting*, and between meetings they compete, cheat and
occasionally murder each other's people.

**What they want:** a stable wholesale price and a predictable freight rate.
Genuinely, that is all. Territory is a means. Terror is a marketing expense.

**How they fund themselves:** Nine. Roughly 380 kg a month across all members.

**How they fight:** Two utterly different tiers, and the difference is legible.

- *Plaza gunmen* — young, over-armed, badly trained, paid by the week. High
  volume of fire, appalling cover discipline, fight from doorways and truck
  beds, advance when they should not. They are dangerous in the way a car crash
  is dangerous.
- *Retainers* — perhaps eight men in the whole Basin, ex-military, extremely
  well paid, on personal contract to a principal. Quiet, patient, will let the
  gunmen die to establish where the player is. These are the ones who kill you.

The Table does not *want* a firefight at a business location. Once one starts,
they escalate past all proportion, because reputation is the only asset that
actually secures their supply chain. They mutilate and display. This is not
sadism for its own sake — it is **advertising**, aimed at their own workforce.

**Civilians:** instrumental. Staff are protected because staff are expensive to
replace and because a principal's household is a matter of standing. Everyone
else is leverage. The Table will execute one person in front of forty to save
having to threaten the forty individually, and considers this efficient.

**Who they hate:** Kirravai, absolutely, because Kirravai burns kitchens and
cannot be bought. The Rungs, contemptuously. Margrave, warily. The Pilotage, the
way a manufacturer hates a shipping line — constantly, professionally, and
without ever quite doing anything about it.

**Colour identity:** warm brass / ochre `#c8a355`, the project accent. Gold is
the point.

**Voice:** Bravado, numbers, insult. Members address each other by number
("Six, come up") because names are evidence. Loud. Laughs in the wrong places.

---

### 6.2 THE PILOTAGE — `syndicate`

> *Formally: the Kavesk Pilots' Association, chartered 1884, holder of the
> statutory monopoly on harbour and river pilotage in Alluvine waters.*

**What it actually is:** a 140-year-old guild that has converted a legal
monopoly on *guiding ships* into control of everything that moves. It is a
family business — nine extended families, membership by apprenticeship, a
written constitution, and an annual dinner. It is also the largest smuggling
organisation on the coast.

**What they want:** volume and predictability. They are agnostic about cargo.
They will move Nine, potash, ammunition, refrigerated vaccine and people in the
same week, and their rate card distinguishes between them only by risk premium.

**How they fund themselves:** freight, pilotage fees, warehousing, the customs
arrangement, and a per-tonne levy at the Cut.

**How they fight:** as a business protecting inventory. **Ambush and
disengage.** Prepared positions with pre-planned withdrawal routes. Roadblocks.
Boats. Cranes used as weapons in ways the player will not expect. They set a
trap, spring it, take what they came for, and are gone inside ninety seconds —
and if the trap fails they leave immediately rather than reinforce, because men
are assets and assets are on a balance sheet.

They are the best night fighters in the Basin below the professional tier,
because half of them have been running unlit boats since they were twelve.

They use **smoke to leave**, not to advance. Remember that; it is a signature.

**Civilians:** bought, not coerced. The Pilotage pays for silence, employs half
the Cut's teenagers as lookouts, funds two schools, and is the only faction that
will genuinely compensate a family for a death it caused. It will also drown an
informer, personally, and everyone knows which families that has happened to.

**Who they hate:** nobody, professionally. They dislike Margrave for raising
insurance costs and the Rungs for renegotiating. Their real fear is that the
Alluvine Republic will one day actually enforce its own customs law, which would
end them in a week.

**Colour identity:** harbour green-teal `#2f6b63`. Working paint, not a flag.

**Voice:** procedural, freight vocabulary, quiet. Boats have names and men are
called by their boat. "Cargo's hot. Break it off."

---

### 6.3 KIRRAVAI — `insurgent`

> *kirre* (brother, kin) + *vai* (water; also: to return).
> Outsiders translate it "the Returners." Members translate it "we who are
> owed."

**What it actually is:** a millenarian movement, thirty-eight years old, grown
out of the three drowned towns. Its founder, **Sevran Ollant Kirre**, was a dam
technician who walked off the site in 1986 and did not stop walking for two
years. Its doctrine is short enough to memorise, and most members have:

> *The water was taken. The debt was not paid. Every light burning in the Basin
> burns on the roof of Tenkai. We will not be paid in light.*

This is not a real-world religion and does not resemble one. It is a **debt
theology**: the world owes a specific, calculable sum to a specific, named group
of dead people, and until it is paid, nothing bought with that debt is
legitimate. Electricity from the dam is stolen property. A man who strings the
line is a receiver of stolen goods. A man who pays the switchyard for power is
buying from a thief and is himself a thief.

They are entirely sincere and completely implacable, and they run the only
functioning clinics and schools in three valleys.

**What they want:** the dam decommissioned and the reservoir drained. Nothing
less has ever been offered and nothing less is negotiable. Everything else — the
attacks on the Table's kitchens, the taxation of the road, the executions — is
instrumental.

**How they fund themselves:** a tithe, collected openly and receipted; a road
tax on the western valleys which even the Table pays; and the sale of protection
to villages that have no other protection. They are poor and they do not steal
from the people they govern, which is a large part of why they are still here.

**How they fight:** **From terrain, at range, patiently.**

- Old bolt-action rifles and a small number of very good scoped weapons.
  Marksmanship as a discipline, ammunition as a scarce sacrament.
- IEDs on the power roads, in culverts, in the fourteen curves below the Gate.
  This is their signature and it is *very* effective.
- Cells of four to six, operating semi-autonomously.
- **No radios.** By doctrine — the network is powered by the dam, and using it
  is participating in the theft. They communicate by whistle, mirror, and
  runner. The tactical consequence is precise and should be modelled exactly:
  *alert propagation within a cell is instant; between cells it is slow (two to
  four minutes); but once it has propagated it is total and permanent.* You can
  kill a cell silently and have three minutes. You cannot ever un-alert a
  valley.
- They do not break under suppression the way other people do, because being
  pinned is not, to them, a losing condition.
- **They do not surrender.** Not tactically, not ever. Cornered fighters
  advance.

**Civilians:** protected, fed, taxed, educated — and collaborators are executed
after a hearing that is, by the standards of the region, scrupulously fair. Both
of those things are true at once. Kirravai is the faction most likely to save a
wounded civilian and the faction most likely to hang one.

**Who they hate:** the Ustari Watch, above everything — gravediggers who guard
the grave. Then the Table, whose kitchens run on stolen water. Margrave, whom
they regard as bailiffs. The Pilotage, transactionally.

**Colour identity:** slate indigo `#3a4a63`. No insignia; they are recognised by
what they do not carry.

**Voice:** calm, liturgical, formal. Names, always — never numbers, never
nicknames. They speak of water, of debt, of what is owed. They will tell you
what they are about to do before doing it.

### 6.4 THE RUNGS — `militia`

> *Formally: the Ketturai Ward Committees. On the walls, in stolen highway
> paint: a ladder with a broken rung.*

**What it actually is:** the Cut's self-defence committees, formed during the
1994 cholera year to stop the removals, hardened over thirty years into
something halfway between a neighbourhood watch and a protection racket. There
are nineteen wards. Each ward has its own committee, its own money, and its own
opinion of the others.

**What they want:** to not be moved again. Every generation of the Cut has been
moved once — off the terraces, out of the drowned valleys, off the lower
cutting when the rail widened. The Rungs exist so that there is no next time.

**How they fund themselves:** ward levies, a cut of retail, "passage" charged on
vehicles, and money from the Pilotage for lookout work.

**How they fight:** **They swarm and they scatter.**

- Numerous. Badly armed — sawn shotguns, pistol-calibre carbines, and the
  KOW "Cut gun," a crude open-bolt 9 mm that costs less than a phone.
- Sixteen to twenty-six years old. Some are younger, and the game should not
  hide that.
- Extraordinary local knowledge. Roof routes, holes knocked between buildings,
  ladders, drains. They will appear behind the player from a direction that was
  not, forty seconds ago, a direction.
- **Phones.** Everyone in the Cut has a phone. Alert propagation is
  near-instant and area-wide, and it does not require line of sight. The player
  cannot fight quietly in the Cut. That is the design intent.
- Contact is short. Three or four rounds, then gone. Then again, from above.
- Morale is brittle in the moment and irrelevant over time: kill two and the
  rest break; twenty minutes later there are eleven more.

**Civilians:** they *are* the civilians. The distinction is a weapon in the
hand, and sometimes not even that. A boy with a phone on a roof is doing
military work. Whether he is a combatant is a question the game should let the
player answer with a trigger and then live with.

**Who they hate:** situational. They resent the Pilotage's rates, take the
Table's money and despise them for it, and have no opinion about Kirravai at
all. What unites the nineteen wards is a shared certainty that everyone above
900 m regards the Cut as a problem to be cleared.

**Colour identity:** acid green `#8fbf3f` — the highway paint they steal, which
is the cheapest paint in the Basin.

**Voice:** young, fast, slang-heavy, overlapping. Nicknames. Half of a
conversation is on a phone to somebody two streets away. They shout warnings to
each other constantly and half of them are wrong.

### 6.5 MARGRAVE — `pmc`

> *Margrave Risk Partners LLP. Registered Jersey. Sole in-Basin client:
> Meridian Concession Holdings SA. Personnel in country: 340.*
> *A margrave was the officer who held the march — the border — for a lord who
> was somewhere else.*

**What it actually is:** a professional military contractor whose mandate is
**evidentiary, not territorial**. Margrave is not in the Basin to win a war. It
is in the Basin to maintain a documented, dated, photographed, continuously
staffed physical presence on eleven concession parcels, so that when the
tribunal sits in fourteen months, the company's counsel can demonstrate
uninterrupted possession.

Read that again, because it determines everything about how they fight.

**What they want:** to complete the contract without an incident that produces
adverse footage or a claim. They would rather lose a parcel briefly and retake
it on schedule than win a firefight that generates a coroner's report.

**How they fund themselves:** a contract, billed monthly, drawn against
Meridian Concession Holdings' litigation budget rather than any operational
budget. This is a real distinction and it is why they are lawyered rather than
led.

**How they fight:** **By the book, and the book is very good.**

- Genuine fire and movement. Pairs bound; one moves, one watches; sectors are
  assigned and held.
- Radio discipline. Short transmissions, callsigns, contact reports with
  direction, range and description.
- Night-vision and thermal as standard, which makes the dark theirs. Fighting
  Margrave at 0200 is a different and much worse proposition than at noon.
- Deliberate clearing. They do not rush a building. They will spend twenty
  minutes on a structure the Rungs would enter at a run.
- Casualty collection. They will break contact to recover a man, and they will
  do it competently.
- **They withdraw.** At roughly 25% casualties, or when the engagement exceeds
  the contract's risk envelope, Margrave disengages in good order — covering,
  bounding, taking their wounded and their cameras. They log it. It is not
  cowardice; it is compliance, and it makes them the only enemy in the Basin the
  player can *out-persist* rather than out-shoot.
- Body cameras. All of them, all the time. The footage is the product.

**Civilians:** strictly by rules of engagement, and genuinely so, because the
footage is discoverable. Margrave gives real verbal warnings and means them.
They will let an armed man walk away if he is walking away. They are, measured
by civilian deaths per contact, by far the least harmful armed group in the
Basin — and they are here to secure a mineral title for a shell company, and
they will kill the player without hesitation for standing on Parcel 41.

That contradiction is the point of them.

**Who they hate:** nobody, and that is unsettling. They have an understanding
with the Pilotage (cargo is not their business), an open standing contract
against Kirravai (who attack the parcels), a nervous mutual tolerance with the
Table, and they charge the Ustari Watch rent, in cash, monthly, to leave the dam
alone.

**Colour identity:** pale steel `#9aa5ad`. Muted, expensive, no insignia larger
than a name tape.

**Voice:** clipped procedure. Callsigns and alphanumerics ("Two-Four, contact
front"). ROE language spoken out loud, partly to the target and partly to the
camera. Accents from six countries.

### 6.6 THE USTARI WATCH — `militia`

> *There is no formal name. The payroll line, which has not been funded since
> 2009, reads "Site Security, Ustari Gate (non-establishment)."*

**What it actually is:** the dam's own workforce, armed. About 280 men,
average age forty-seven — turbine fitters, linemen, crane operators, and now
their sons. They have held one perimeter for twenty-two years, unpaid, because
the dam is why their valley eats.

**What they want:** the dam to keep running and their village to keep existing.
Nothing else. They have no ambitions beyond the wire and no interest in the
Basin's politics, which they regard as something that happens to other people.

**How they fund themselves:** they sell power. Cash, at the switchyard, per
megawatt-hour, to the Table, the Pilotage, the works at Kavesk, and — this is
the part they do not discuss — indirectly to the kitchens that are killing their
own grandchildren. They know. It has been raised at meetings. They have voted on
it twice and voted, both times, to keep selling.

**How they fight:** **They do not move.**

- Prepared, dug-in positions built up over two decades. Sandbagged, roofed,
  with registered arcs and range cards.
- Two elderly Kilbride belt-fed machine guns on the crest with pre-plotted
  fields of fire that have been rehearsed since 2003.
- Mines and wire on the two approach routes. Everyone local knows where.
  Everyone local walks the long way.
- Searchlights, and the willingness to use them and stand in the dark.
- They shoot **late and accurately**. Fire discipline is the one thing they are
  genuinely excellent at, because ammunition has to be bought.
- They will not pursue. Break contact and they let you go, and then they call
  their families. Off the site they are nothing — a Watch fighter fifty metres
  outside the wire is an unarmoured man in his fifties in the open.
- **They will parley.** Every faction has, at some point, walked up to the wire
  under a white cloth and been received politely. Missions that require the
  player to refuse a parley should feel exactly as bad as they are.

**Civilians:** they are their own civilians. The village is inside the wire. So
are the children, and the school, and this constrains every plan anyone makes
against the dam — which the Watch understands perfectly and exploits without
embarrassment.

**Who they hate:** Kirravai, with a bitterness that has become the organising
principle of their lives. Kirravai calls them gravediggers. They call Kirravai
"the drowned," which is worse. Both sides have men who lost the same family in
1984 and made opposite choices about it.

**Colour identity:** oxide orange `#b4552d` — the primer on the penstocks, the
paint on the crane, the colour of the dam.

**Voice:** laconic, middle-aged, work vocabulary. Marrek given names. They talk
to each other the way men talk who have worked the same shift for thirty years.
"Hold your arc, Tolu. Don't chase him."

### 6.8 Civilians

Not a faction, but they outnumber all six combined and the game should never
forget it. In the Cut they are the population; at Verrick they are staff; at
Ondai there are two elderly caretakers who were on the university payroll and
never left; at the Gate they are families inside the wire; on the flat they are
truck drivers.

Civilians in the Basin are not helpless and they are not innocent bystanders in
some abstract way. They are people making the least-bad available choice, at
scale, for decades. They will hide the player and they will sell the player, and
which one depends on who last did them harm.

## 7. THE PLAYER

### 7.1 Who you work for

You are contracted through the **Halloran Trust for Basin Development** — a
donor-funded development organisation headquartered in a converted freight
office in Kavesk. The Trust runs eleven clinics, a water programme, a school
feeding scheme and the only ambulance service above 900 m. Its annual report is
published, audited and honest. Its people are, for the most part, good at their
jobs and decent about it.

It also has a security annex. The security annex has a budget line called
**"Facility Protection, Non-Standard."**

That is you.

You will never meet anyone from the Trust in person. You deal with a desk. The
desk's callsign is **CANNERY**, and the voice on it belongs to a woman who calls
herself **Deverell** — no rank, no first name, no small talk, flat delivery, and
a habit of ending transmissions with *"Nothing further."* She is well-informed,
occasionally protective, and she is not telling you everything, and she has
never once claimed to be.

### 7.2 What you are paid in

- **$1,150 per active day**, wired to an account at Ostrand Marine Bank in
  Kavesk. Ostrand Marine also holds the Pilotage's freight accounts and two of
  the Table's shipping companies. There is exactly one bank.
- **A recovery share**: 12% of cash and materiel recovered, *net of disposal
  costs.* Disposal costs are whatever the desk says they are, and you have no
  standing to audit them.
- **A completion bonus at contract end, month 18.** It is roughly four times
  everything else combined and it pays only if the contract completes.

That last item is the leash and everybody involved knows it.

### 7.3 The leash

Four things hold you:

1. **The permit.** You are in the country legally, on an Alluvine work permit
   describing you as a *facility risk surveyor*, issued to a name that is not
   yours, renewed every ninety days. It is renewed by the person who gives you
   orders. Without it you are an undocumented foreign national in possession of
   a rifle, and the Alluvine sentence for that is fourteen years.
2. **The bonus.** Eighteen months, or nothing.
3. **The absence of any other exit.** There is one road and one airstrip, and
   you do not control either.
4. **The paperwork you signed.** There is a repatriation-of-remains clause.
   There is a no-negotiation clause. There is a prewritten public statement
   describing you, in the event of capture, as *"an individual with no
   relationship to this organisation."* You have read it. It is accurate. That
   is what makes it work.

### 7.4 What you are

A survey instrument that can shoot.

Every operation you run generates a file: date, grid, disposition of forces,
photographs, a condition assessment of the site. Those files are collated. They
are *very* professionally collated. And somewhere in month nine you will notice
that the collation format matches the annexure schedule of an arbitration
tribunal, and that the parcels you have been clearing are numbered rather than
named.

The game never confirms why you took this job. Three explanations are canon-
compatible and the player may choose to believe any of them: a claim that was
refused, a hearing you cannot attend, or the simple fact that the money is
real and the alternative was not. What the game *does* confirm is that you
knew, roughly, what this was, and came anyway.

### 7.5 The slow burn

The arc is not "your employer is secretly evil." It is quieter and worse than
that.

The Halloran Trust is genuinely doing good. Its clinics are real. Its water
programme has measurably reduced infant mortality in the Cut. And three seats on
its eleven-member board are held by nominees of Meridian Concession Holdings SA,
because the company endowed the Trust in 2006 as part of a reputational
settlement, and endowments come with seats.

Nobody had to conspire. The institution simply has a shape, and the shape has a
direction, and every operation you run points that way. Your work degrades the
Basin's armed groups, which degrades the Basin's economy, which degrades the
valuation the tribunal will place on a concession the company lost — and reduces
a nine-figure award against the company to something manageable.

You are performing due diligence with a rifle.

The last question the campaign asks is not *"who are you working for?"* — you
will have worked that out by month ten. It is: **now that you know, what is the
difference between leaving and staying?** The Trust's clinics still run either
way. The bonus pays only if you finish. And the man who takes the contract after
you will be worse at it.

## 8. NAMING CONVENTIONS

**Other teams: conform to this.** Names are the cheapest and most visible way
this world holds together or falls apart.

### 8.1 The three strata

Every place in the Basin has names from one or more of three historical layers.
Using the right one is characterisation.

| Stratum | Origin | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Marrek** | Pre-1889 highland tongue | Doubled consonants, `au/ai/ua` diphthongs, glottal `'`, final `-ai -un -esk -oa` | Ustari, Ketturai, Ka'sun, Ondai, Sarrahun, Tenkai, Ollant, Sevra, Kavesk, Tulmar |
| **Concession** | 1889–1958 company | European surnames, industrial nouns, **numbers** | Halloran, Verrick, Dessin, Mott, Coyle, Ashgrave, Pell, Brannoc, Reach Four, Number Six Lift, the Nitrate Works |
| **Administrative** | 1958– state & BIA | Dry, bureaucratic, hyphenated, numbered | Transit Corridor 2, Ka'sun Administrative Zone, Parcel 41, Sector B-North |

**Rules:**

- **Two names minimum** for anything significant. The map label uses the
  Concession name; radio chatter uses the Marrek name; official documents use
  the administrative one. *"They call it Reach Four. Nobody who lives there has
  ever called it that."*
- **No Spanish, no Portuguese, no Arabic, no Slavic place names.** Not one. If a
  name would be at home in a real conflict zone, change it.
- **No saints, no "Santa/San/Nuevo/Puerto/Villa."** The chartering power was a
  joint-stock company, not a crown, and it was secular and cheap.
- **Numbers are a company signature.** Reaches, Lifts, Parcels, Sidings. If it
  was built to make money before 1958, it has a number.

### 8.2 Coining new Marrek names

Syllable inventory — combine two or three:

`ka · ke · ku · ta · te · tu · ma · me · mu · sa · se · su · va · ve · vu ·`
`ol · or · un · esk · ai · au · ua · rra · llo · tt · nn · hun · rai · vek · dai`

Valid: **Ketturai, Sevrai, Ollantesk, Tulvai, Ka'mun, Serrahu, Vekkun, Maurai,
Tessun, Ondai, Kirrau, Ammatai.**
Invalid: anything that reads as an existing real-world place. Say it out loud
first.

### 8.3 Operation codenames

The Trust's cryptonym convention: **[material] + [trade term]**, both mundane,
both drawn from closed dictionaries, always upper case, never evocative of the
operation's content. Real cryptonym systems are deliberately meaningless.

*Materials:* ASH · BRASS · CHALK · CINDER · COPPER · FLINT · GLASS · LEAD ·
LIME · PEWTER · PITCH · SLATE · TALLOW · TIN · VELLUM · WAX · WICKER · ZINC

*Trade terms (carpentry, weaving, shipping):* BOBBIN · BOLLARD · CAULK ·
DOVETAIL · FERRULE · GANTRY · GROMMET · HAWSER · JOINER · KEELSON · LINTEL ·
MORTISE · PURLIN · RIGGER · SELVAGE · SHUTTLE · SPLICE · TILLER · TRANSOM ·
WARP · WEFT · WINCH

Yields **LEAD DOVETAIL**, **ASH BOBBIN**, **CHALK TILLER**, **ZINC SELVAGE**.
396 combinations, all of them flat, none of them cool. That is correct.

**Forbidden:** predators, mythology, weather, Greek and Norse anything, and any
word that sounds like it was chosen by someone who was enjoying himself.
No OPERATION IRON TALON. Ever.

### 8.4 Player callsigns

Assigned per contract, burned afterward like a phone number. One word,
domestic or architectural, deliberately drab:

LATCH · GABLE · MANTEL · SASH · NEWEL · PANTRY · KETTLE · TRIVET · BRACKET ·
DORMER · PLINTH · JAMB · SILL · CORNICE · SOFFIT · TREAD · RISER · VALANCE ·
AWNING · SHUTTER · PICKET · CULVERT · GUTTER · HEARTH

Desk and station callsigns are storage buildings: **CANNERY** (the Trust ops
desk), DRY DOCK, SALT HOUSE, BONDED STORE, CUSTOM HOUSE, COLD STORE.

### 8.5 Faction internal handles

A faction's address style is a fingerprint. Audio and AI teams should treat this
as load-bearing:

| Faction | Style | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Table | Numbers | *"Six. Six, come up."* |

*Marrek family names:* Ollant, Kirre, Tessun, Vekkun, Ammarai, Serrahun, Maurai

## 9. ARMS INDUSTRY

Twelve in-fiction manufacturers. The weapons and weapon-model teams should draw
every weapon in the game from this list; nothing in the Basin was made by a
company that exists in reality.

Each entry gives the **character** (which should show in the mesh, the handling
numbers and the sound) and the **model-naming convention**.

---

**1. BRISCOE DEFENSE** — *North American. Family firm, 1911, now a listed
conglomerate.*
The ubiquitous AR-pattern. Nobody's favourite and everybody's rifle. Adequate
triggers, indifferent finish, an aftermarket the size of a small economy.
Briscoe's real product is *availability*: there are more Briscoe uppers in the
Basin than there are functioning telephones.
**Models:** `BD-` + number, occasionally with a marketing suffix nobody uses.
BD-16, BD-11, BD-16 Patrol.
**Character:** middle of every stat block. Enormous slot count. Boring, and the
player will end up using one anyway.

**2. ZAGREN ARSENAL** — *State factory, large non-aligned industrial power.*
Stamped steel, chrome-lined, sold by the container. Long-stroke piston, brutal
ergonomics, will fire after being buried for a year. Everything Zagren makes was
designed to be operated by a conscript in a hurry.
**Models:** `Type ##`. Type 62 (7.62 intermediate), Type 74 (5.45 analogue),
Type 9 (rocket launcher), Type 41 (pistol).
**Character:** heavy, loud, reliable, coarse sights, terrible ergonomics number,
best-in-class durability. High recoil, forgiving of neglect.

**3. HALDEN & VOSS** — *Northern European. Privately held. Expensive.*
CNC and polymer, roller-delayed heritage, tolerances measured because they can
be. Proprietary rail interfaces and a documented contempt for the aftermarket:
H&V's position is that the rifle left the factory correct.
**Models:** `HV-` + number + variant letter. HV-7A3, HV-4, HV-12 (suppressed
9 mm).
**Character:** best ergonomics and recoil recovery in the game, fewest slots,
highest price, low durability tolerance for dirt. The professional's rifle.

**4. BOREAL SMALL ARMS** — *Sub-arctic. Barrel makers who drifted into rifles.*
Understated, no marketing budget, and the finest barrels made anywhere. Wooden
furniture on the hunting line, composite on the military. Every Boreal weapon
shoots better than its owner.
**Models:** single cold nouns — Boreal **Fell**, **Sleet**, **Hoar**, **Rime**,
**Drift**.
**Character:** minimum spread MOA, long barrels, heavy, slow handling, few fire
modes. DMR and sniper backbone.

**5. CORVAINE ARMEMENT** — *State-owned, Francophone tradition, export-driven.*
Bullpups and vehicle weapons. Elegant, clever, ergonomically eccentric, and
adopted almost exclusively by countries that received them as part of a
treaty package. The Marrek Confederacy bought 4,000 in 1991 and nobody liked
them.
**Models:** `CR-` + number. CR-5, CR-9, CR-21 (vehicle).
**Character:** compact for barrel length, awkward reloads, odd balance,
surprisingly good accuracy. Fielded by the Ustari Watch because of that 1991
deal.

**6. ORSILLO** — *Mediterranean family house, founded 1782. Shotguns.*
Nine generations. Game guns for people with estates, and a tactical line the
family is faintly embarrassed by. The engraving on the receiver of a sporting
Orsillo is done by hand, which is why a sawn-off one in the Cut is such an
obscenity.
**Models:** named — Orsillo **Vespro**, **Marea**, **Corda**, **Bracco**.
**Character:** fast-handling semi-auto and over-under 12 gauge. The Rungs saw
them off; the Table gilds them.

**7. ARDEN SYSTEMS** — *Small state, enormous export industry.*
Submachine guns and PDWs for close protection. Folding, compact, ambidextrous,
designed around getting a weapon out from under a jacket. Every bodyguard on
earth has held one.
**Models:** `AS-` + number. AS-3, AS-7, AS-12K.
**Character:** highest ergonomics, low recoil, weak terminal ballistics, tiny
sight radius. Table retainers and Pilotage boat crews.

**8. PELLAND** — *Alpine. Precision pistols. Police contracts.*
Striker-fired, obsessively consistent, and the only handgun maker anyone in the
Basin will name unprompted. A Pelland is what you carry if somebody else is
paying.
**Models:** `PL-` + two digits. PL-19, PL-24, PL-45.
**Character:** best-in-class pistol accuracy and trigger, unexciting capacity,
premium price.

**9. KILBRIDE ORDNANCE** — *Old-world, 1890s heritage. Belt-feds and mounts.*
Machine guns, naval mounts, vehicle rings. Built like bridge sections. Half the
Kilbrides in the Basin predate the Basin War and several predate the dam.
**Models:** `K` + number + `Mk` + number. K7 Mk4, K12 Mk2.
**Character:** heavy, tripod-hungry, enormous sustained fire, hopeless mobility.
The Ustari Watch's crest guns are K7 Mk4s from 1979.

**10. KAVESK ORDNANCE WORKS (KOW)** — *Local. The Concession's own plant.*
Built 1913 to make blasting caps and cartridges for the mines, never quite
closed. Today KOW makes reloaded ball ammunition of variable quality (*"salted
brass"*), a stamped 7.62 carbine called the **Lift**, and the **Cut gun** — an
open-bolt 9 mm submachine gun assembled in workshops across the settlement from
KOW parts kits, which costs less than a phone and fires until it doesn't.
**Models:** Lift-2, Lift-3, "Cut gun" (no official designation).
**Character:** worst durability and spread in the game, cheapest, everywhere.
The Cut gun should feel genuinely nasty to use: no sights worth the name,
violent, and it will jam.

**11. BRACHE MUNITIONS** — *Continental. Premium ammunition.*
Match, armour-piercing, subsonic. If a round in the Basin actually performs to
spec, it is a Brache round and somebody paid for it. Margrave buys Brache
exclusively; everyone else buys KOW and hopes.
**Models:** caliber + load code. `7.62×51 BR-AP`, `9×19 BR-SD`.

**12. VEDETTE OPTIQUE** *(optics)* and **NORTHWATCH SYSTEMS** *(night/thermal)*.
Vedette makes day glass — prisms, LPVOs, a fine 6× that half the Basin's
marksmen carry a battered example of. Northwatch makes image intensifiers and
thermals, is subject to export control, and is therefore the single clearest
tell of who is funding whom: **a man with a working Northwatch device did not
buy it himself.**
Suppressors come from **QUILLAN SOUND**, and they are rare enough that the
sound of a suppressed shot in the Basin is itself intelligence.

### 9.1 Who fields what

| Faction | Primary | Secondary | Optics / night |
|---|---|---|---|

## 10. STYLE GUIDE FOR EVERYONE WRITING IN THIS WORLD

Nine rules. They are all the same rule.

1. **Specificity over atmosphere.** "A drug lab in the jungle" is nothing. "Forty
   kilos a month of BX-9 out of a herbarium at 1,650 m" is a place.
2. **Bureaucracy is the horror.** The most frightening documents in this world
   are correctly formatted. A budget line, a parcel number, a renewal date.
3. **Nobody is having fun.** Not the enemies, not the handler, not the player.
   Delete any line that sounds like it was written to be quoted.
4. **No one-word villains.** Every armed group in this world is solving a real
   problem in a way that creates a worse one. If you can summarise a faction as
   "the bad guys," you have written it wrong.
5. **The violence is loud, brief and squalid.** No duels. No standoffs with
   dialogue. People die in under two seconds, badly, and usually facing the
   wrong way.
6. **Count the civilians.** The campaign layer tracks civilian deaths and the
   game shows the number. That number is the score that matters and the player
   should never be congratulated on it.
7. **Never explain the politics in dialogue.** Characters do not brief each
   other on things they both know. Politics arrives as an inconvenience: a road
   closed, a rate changed, a man who used to be reachable and isn't.
8. **Restraint in the prose.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No adjective
   that is doing emotional work the facts could do.
9. **Nothing in Meridian Basin is a metaphor for a real place.** If a line would
   land differently for someone from a specific real country, rewrite it.
