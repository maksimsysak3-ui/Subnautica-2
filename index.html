  • Your task: bring the three fire lookout towers back into service. Each one is missing something — the forest will tell you where to look.
  • Campfires can be lit at established camps. Rest beside one to sleep until dawn; your expedition is recorded there.
  • Follow the compass markers. Diamonds are your current objective; triangles are towers you've discovered.
  • Wildlife is shy. Crouch of patience — walk, don't sprint — and you'll get close enough for a photograph.
  • Ranger June Calloway kept a journal her final season. Her pages are still out there, weighted under stones.
  • Supply caches raise your maximum stamina. Look for small crates near landmarks.
  • The forest changes with the hour. Some things only happen after dark.

Credits

Emberwood was written by Claude as a single HTML file — every system handcrafted from scratch, with no game engine, no libraries, and no external assets of any kind.

The 3D renderer is raw WebGL2: hand-rolled matrix math, custom GLSL shaders, cascading day/night lighting, dynamic shadow mapping, and GPU-instanced forests. The terrain, every tree, rock, tower, and creature is procedural geometry built vertex by vertex. The soundscape — wind, birdsong, crickets, fire, and the score itself — is synthesized live by a handwritten Web Audio engine. Nothing was downloaded; nothing is borrowed.

Written for one player, one forest, and however many sunrises you care to watch.

“Look after the quiet places.” — J.C.
